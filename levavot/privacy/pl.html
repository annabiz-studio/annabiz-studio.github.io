In short

  • Your hearts live on your phone. Your messages, photos, voice recordings, reminders and settings are saved only in the app's private storage on your device. We (the developer) do not receive them. You can also save a copy as a file wherever you choose (Export and restore); that file is yours and we never see it.
  • This version has no server and no internet permission, so the app cannot send your hearts, photos or recordings anywhere by itself. There is no account, no ads, no analytics, no tracking, and nothing is shared with third parties.
  • Voice hearts and your phone's speech service. The app turns your recording into text with Android's speech recognition. When your phone has an on-device recognizer, the app uses it. When it does not, Android's default speech service handles it, and on most phones that is Google's, which may process the audio on Google's servers under Google's policy. See Voice and transcription.
  • Android's own backup. If backup is on in your phone's settings, Android may copy the app's data (including your hearts, photos and voice recordings) to your own Google account, and move it to a new phone when you transfer. We cannot access it. See When information leaves your device.
  • For married couples aged 18 and over.

Who we are

Levavot ("Hearts") is published by AnnaBiz ("we", "us"). For anything about privacy, write to annabiz.studio@gmail.com. Levavot currently has no ads and no purchases. If that changes, we will update this policy first.

What is stored on your device only

  • Your hearts: the text, the type of heart, stickers, color, pattern and font, the date you marked, a place name you typed (free text; the app does not use your location), when the heart was created and opened, which lists it belongs to, and the link to the heart it replies to. They are saved in a file in the app's private storage.
  • Photos: up to 3 per heart, scaled down to at most 1280 pixels on the long side, saved in the app's private storage.
  • Voice recordings: sound files of up to 60 seconds, saved in the app's private storage.
  • Reminders, wedding date and settings: the reminders you create are saved with your hearts. Your wedding date (optional: only if you choose to enter it, used only for an anniversary reminder, and deleted when you turn it off), display choices, notification settings and the times of the app's own gentle suggestions are kept in the app's local settings.
  • Weekly check-in: about once a week, after the first week of use, the app asks one question ("This week, did I feel listened to and respected?"). You answer with one tap (three faces) or skip. Your answers, with the time you gave them, are stored only on your phone, in the app's local settings. The app never sends them anywhere, and your spouse does not see them. Like your other settings, they are part of Android's backup if backup is on (see below).
  • "The app learns you": the hour at which you usually send hearts is worked out on your phone, from the times of your last hearts only. The app does not read or analyze what your hearts say, and none of this is sent anywhere.

All of this is deleted when you clear the app's storage or uninstall it. Hearts are never deleted automatically; only you delete them.

Features and the permissions they use

Microphone and transcription: voice hearts (RECORD_AUDIO)

The first time you record a voice heart, the app explains why it needs the microphone and then asks for permission. It uses the microphone only while you are recording (up to 60 seconds). The recording is saved as a sound file in the app's private storage and attached to the heart. Levavot has no internet permission and does not send the recording anywhere itself.

The written transcript. On Android 13 and later, while you record, the app also asks Android's speech recognition to turn your words into text, so they appear inside the heart (you can edit them). The transcript is made on the phone when the phone can do it, and otherwise by the phone's speech service (usually Google's), as described below. The short explanation the app shows before asking for the microphone says the same.

Android 12 and older. Automatic transcription needs Android 13 or later (and a speech recognition service on the phone). Before you record, the app shows a note saying that this phone has no automatic transcription. The recording is still saved, and you type the text yourself.

  • When your phone has an on-device speech recognizer, the app asks for that one, and also asks for offline recognition. The audio is then processed on the phone.
  • When your phone has no on-device recognizer, Android's default speech recognition service is used. On most phones that is Google's speech service. That service may then send the audio to its provider's servers to be transcribed, under the provider's own privacy policy, usually the Google Privacy Policy, and not under this one. We cannot control it, and we do not receive the audio or the text from it.
  • Recording a voice heart is optional. If you do not want your voice processed by a speech service, write your heart instead of recording it.

Photos

You choose photos with Android's system photo picker. Levavot receives only the photos you choose. It has no access to the rest of your photos and does not ask for photo-library or camera permission. When you delete a heart, its photos and voice recording files are deleted too.

Notifications and reminders (POST_NOTIFICATIONS, SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM, RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED)

Reminders you set, the daily encouragement, reminders for important dates and the app's own gentle suggestions to send a heart are scheduled and shown locally on your phone. They are not messages from a server. By default the app sends at most 3 of its own suggestions a week; you can change the number or turn them off. Precise timing (exact alarms) is optional; without it, reminders arrive within a few minutes of the chosen time. When Android does not allow exact alarms for the app (by default this is the case on Android 14 and later), the Reminders screen shows a line you can tap to open Android's "Alarms & reminders" screen, where you can allow it. That is your choice and the app works without it. After the phone restarts, the app schedules your reminders again.

Lock screen. A notification can contain the text of the heart it points to (the daily encouragement and important dates) or the text of a reminder you wrote. By default these notifications are marked private: while the phone is locked they show only a general line, "A reminder from Levavot" or "A heart is waiting in Levavot", without the text, and the full text appears after you unlock the phone. You can change this with "Hide content on the lock screen" in the app's Reminders screen. The app's own gentle suggestions use general wording and never contain the text of a heart. How a private notification looks on a locked phone also follows Android's own lock-screen notification settings, which you can change in Android settings.

Floating bubble over other apps (SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW, FOREGROUND_SERVICE)

If you turn it on yourself in the app's settings, the app first shows a short explanation of what the bubble does and which permission it needs, and you can cancel there. Then, if you allow "Display over other apps" in Android, a small floating heart appears over other apps. It dances when a heart is waiting, opens Levavot when you tap it, and a long press hides it. To keep it running, Android shows a quiet notification while it is on. The bubble is drawn from the app's own data; it does not read, record or send anything about the other apps or what is on your screen. You can turn it off in the app or in Android settings at any time.

Home-screen widget

The widget is a little heart that shows whether a heart is waiting. It does not show the text of your hearts.

Export and restore (your own backup file)

In the app's settings you can save the whole archive as one zip file ("Export the archive") and add hearts back from such a file ("Restore from a file").

  • Export. You choose where the file is saved in Android's system file picker (device storage, or any storage provider you have on your phone). The file contains your hearts and lists, your reminders, and your photos and voice recordings. It is not encrypted or password-protected, so anyone who has the file can read your hearts. Keep it as private as the hearts themselves.
  • Restore. You choose a file in the same file picker. The app adds the hearts and lists that are missing, with their photos and recordings. It only adds: nothing already in the app is replaced or deleted. Reminders and settings are not restored from the file.
  • Nothing goes to us. The export and the restore happen on your phone. The app has no internet permission and sends the file to no cloud of ours. If you pick a cloud storage provider in the file picker, the file goes to that provider by your choice and under its policy, not ours. The file you save is under your control; we cannot see it or delete it.

When information leaves your device

  • Voice hearts: only through your phone's speech service, when it has no on-device recognizer, as described above.
  • Android device backup: the app allows Android's standard backup. If backup is turned on in your phone's settings, Android may copy the app's data to the backup in your own Google account, or to a new phone when you transfer. In Levavot this includes your hearts, photos, voice recordings, reminders and settings (such as your weekly check-in answers), both in the cloud backup and when you transfer to a new phone. On Android 9 and later this backup can be end-to-end encrypted with your screen lock. We cannot access it. You can turn backup off in Android settings, and you can delete the backup from your Google account.
  • An export file you save: only where you choose, as described in Export and restore.
  • Add to calendar: if you tap this button on a heart that has a date, the app opens your phone's calendar app with a new all-day event (the heart's text becomes the title). Nothing is saved until you save it there, and your calendar app and its account (for example Google Calendar) then handle it. Levavot has no calendar permission and does not read your calendar.
  • The app has no links, sharing buttons or network code of its own.

Planned: a relay server (not part of this version)

A future version of Levavot is planned to carry hearts between you and your spouse through a server that only relays them: it is meant to pass each heart on and delete it once it has been delivered, without keeping a backup. That version would need internet access and a way to connect two phones. Nothing of this exists in the current version. If it is released, we will update this policy first, with the details, and publish it before the change takes effect.

What we don't do

  • No ads, no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising ID, no tracking.
  • No access to your location, contacts, camera, calendar or photo library.
  • No accounts, and no server that receives your data. We do not sell or share your data with anyone.
  • We do not read the content of your hearts.

How long data is kept

  • On your device: until you delete it in the app, clear the app's storage or uninstall the app.
  • With us: we receive no data, so there is nothing to keep. E-mails you send us are kept only as long as needed to answer you.
  • In your Android backup: for as long as it exists in your Google account; see Google's settings.
  • In an export file: for as long as you keep the file.

How to delete your data

  • A single heart: delete it from the archive (the app asks you to confirm). Its photos and voice recording are deleted with it.
  • A reminder: delete it in Settings → Reminders.
  • Everything on the phone: Android Settings → Apps → Levavot → Storage → Clear storage, or uninstall the app.
  • Your Android backup: manage or delete it in your phone's backup settings or in your Google account.
  • Export files you saved: delete them yourself where you saved them (we never see them).
  • Weekly check-in answers: they are deleted with the rest of the app's data when you clear its storage or uninstall it.
  • Any question: annabiz.studio@gmail.com. We answer within 30 days. Since we hold no copy of your data, we cannot delete it for you, but we will help you do it yourself.

Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example under the GDPR in the EU/UK, the Israeli Privacy Protection Law or US state laws such as the CCPA), you may have rights over your personal information, such as access, correction, deletion, and objecting to or restricting its processing. Since your data stays only on your phone, you control it directly in the app. You can also complain to your local data-protection authority.

Security

Data on your device is kept in the app's private storage, which Android protects with its app sandbox. The app has no internet permission in this version. No method is 100% secure; we recommend a screen lock on your phone, because your hearts are personal. An export file is not encrypted (see Export and restore), so store it somewhere private.

Children

Levavot is for married couples aged 18 and over and is not directed to children. We do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone under 18. We receive no personal information at all in this version.

Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, for example if we add a server, accounts, a paid plan or ads, we will publish the new version at this address with a new effective date before the change takes effect. If the changes are significant, we will also tell you in the app.
